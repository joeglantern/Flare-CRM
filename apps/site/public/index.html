One afternoon, as the software saw it.
14:02

The phone rings and the customer is already on screen.

Wanjiru Kamau calls the main line. Before anyone lifts the handset, the screen shows her name, her company, who looks after her, her two open deals and the last three things that happened. The agent answers knowing what the call is about.

Works with a Yeastar PBX. Click any number in the product to dial it from your own extension.

14:06

One WhatsApp thread the whole team can see.

She follows up on WhatsApp with a photo of the part. It lands in the same inbox everyone shares, filed under her name, next to the call that just ended. Whoever picks it up sees the whole thread, not their own phone's half of it.

Reply window, templates and delivery ticks are all shown, because Meta enforces them whether you can see them or not.

14:11

Every call, message and note against one customer, in one place.

The agent writes a two line note and it sits on the timeline between the call and the message. Six weeks later, when a different person picks up her next call, the note is still there, in order, with the name of who wrote it.

Contacts, companies, leads, deals, tasks, notes and files. One record per customer, however many people touch it.

14:20

The deal moves, and the board says so.

Her quote goes from Proposal to Negotiation. The board updates for everyone watching it, the value rolls into the forecast, and the stage change is on her timeline with a time and a name.

Pipelines and stages are yours to define. Won and lost each carry a reason.

17:04

At the end of the shift, the numbers are already there.

Calls, answered, missed, talk time, per agent and for the team. The missed calls that nobody called back are a list, not a guess. Everything exports to CSV.

Live calls board for managers while the shift is on. Reports when it is over.

Your own server, your own address

Each company gets its own installation and its own database at yourcompany.flarehub.co.ke, or your own domain. Nobody else's data is in the same table.

An audit log nobody can edit

Every consequential action, including refusals and our own support actions, is written to a log that no administrator can change or delete.

Backups and retention you set

Nightly backups, the last fourteen kept. Recordings and records are deleted on the schedule you choose, by a job that runs whether anyone remembers or not.

Priced per month, in shillings, per company.

A plan is a list of features switched on and a set of limits: people, storage, how long recordings are kept. We agree it with you on a call, then it is signed and enforced on your server.
